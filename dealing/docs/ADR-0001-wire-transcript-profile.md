# ADR-0001: BP52 v1 wire and transcript profile

- Status: proposed; required for the reference implementation
- Scope: implementation-defined details omitted or ambiguous in
  `BP52_DEAL_v1_SPEC.md`

This profile does not claim to amend the research protocol silently. Every
choice below must be ratified into the interoperability specification and
covered by immutable test vectors before a production release.

## Hashes and primitive values

`TaggedSHA256(tag, message)` means exactly:

```text
SHA256(SHA256(ASCII(tag)) || SHA256(ASCII(tag)) || message)
```

Alice is encoded as `0`, Bob as `1`, and all other role bytes are rejected.
Alice owns the lexicographically smaller 32-byte x-only identity key; equal
identity keys are rejected. Attempts begin at zero. Attempt numbers are `u32`
little-endian. Envelope sequence numbers are global within one attempt, begin
at zero, and reset on retry. `low_bit(digest)` is `digest[0] & 1`.

`network_id` is the 32-byte consensus serialization of the network genesis
block hash. `funding_outpoint` is the consensus serialization of the Bitcoin
outpoint: 32-byte transaction ID in wire order followed by `vout` as `u32`
little-endian.

## Canonical attempt schedule

The transcript is linear, so all logically simultaneous flights use the
following canonical role order. An opening is never sent before both associated
commitments have been received and authenticated.

| Sequence | Round | Sender | Payload |
|---:|---:|:---:|---|
| 0 | 0 | A | `KeyCommit` |
| 1 | 0 | B | `KeyCommit` |
| 2 | 1 | A | `KeyOpen` |
| 3 | 1 | B | `KeyOpen` |
| 4 | 2 | A | `KeyProof` |
| 5 | 2 | B | `KeyProof` |
| 6 | 3 | A | `BundleCommit` |
| 7 | 3 | B | `BundleCommit` |
| 8 | 4 | A | `BundleOpen` |
| 9 | 4 | B | `BundleOpen` |
| 10 | 5 | selected first blinder | `BlindFirst` |
| 11 | 6 | other party | `BlindSecond` |
| 12 | 7 | A | `DecryptCommit` |
| 13 | 7 | B | `DecryptCommit` |
| 14 | 8 | A | `DecryptOpen` |
| 15 | 8 | B | `DecryptOpen` |

For the envelope at sequence `n`, `previous_message_hash` is `T_n`. After full
validation:

```text
T_(n+1) = TaggedSHA256(
  "BP52/transcript-msg/v1",
  T_n || Encode(full_envelope_n)
)
```

`T_0` is the attempt-start digest from section 18.3 of the specification. An
incorrect sender, round, type, sequence, predecessor, game, or attempt is
rejected before the transcript advances.

Proofs generated by both parties in one logical flight bind the shared phase
snapshot from before that flight:

- key proofs: `T_4`, after both key openings;
- both player-bundle proofs: `T_6`, after both key proofs;
- first scale proof: `T_10`, after both bundle openings;
- second scale proof: `T_11`, after the first blind message;
- both partial-decryption proofs: `T_12`, before either decryption commitment.

## Payload identifiers and bodies

Payload identifiers are `u16` little-endian:

1. `KeyCommit`: `commitment[32]`
2. `KeyOpen`: `nonce[32] || PK Point32`
3. `KeyProof`: `T Point32 || z Scalar32`
4. `BundleCommit`: `commitment[32]`
5. `BundleOpen`: `nonce[32] || Encode(PlayerBundle)`
6. `BlindFirst`: `Q[108] || E[108](R,S) || ScaleProof`
7. `BlindSecond`: `Q[108] || E[108](R,S) || ScaleProof`
8. `DecryptCommit`: `commitment[32]`
9. `DecryptOpen`: `nonce[32] || Z[108] || PartialDecryptProof`

Fixed arrays carry no element count. Exact proof formats and sizes are:

- key proof: all `T`, then all `z`; 64 bytes;
- encryption-link proof: 9 `(T_V,T_R,T_S)`, then 9
  `(z_v,z_gamma,z_r)`; 1,728 bytes;
- scale proof: 108 `(T_G,T_R,T_S)`, then 108 `z`; 13,824 bytes;
- partial-decryption proof: `T_G`, 108 `T`, `z`; 3,520 bytes.

The hash-length proof has exactly the length derived from the pinned backend
and circuit manifest. Its decoder must consume the full encoding and reproduce
it canonically.

The `PlayerBundle` order is:

```text
role || slots[9](hash || V || R || S) || circuit_id ||
HashLengthProof ByteVector || EncryptionLinkProof ByteVector
```

This follows the required API order in section 23 and never depends on Rust
field layout.

## Commitments and signatures

Key commitments bind only the still-hidden per-attempt public-key share:

```text
TaggedSHA256(
  "BP52/key-commit/v1",
  game_id || attempt || role || nonce || PK
)
```

Both keys are opened and validated before deriving `Y`; the separately
exchanged key proofs can therefore bind `Y` without a dependency cycle.

Decryption commitments are:

```text
TaggedSHA256(
  "BP52/decrypt-commit/v1",
  game_id || attempt || role || nonce || Encode(Z_batch || proof)
)
```

The unsigned envelope encoding follows the field order in section 18.2 through
the length-prefixed payload and omits the signature. The BIP340 message is:

```text
TaggedSHA256("BP52/envelope/v1", Encode(unsigned_envelope))
```

The full envelope appends the 64-byte signature. The decoder applies a global
message cap before allocating a payload and then applies the exact cap for its
payload type.

Bundle commitments use the normative `BP52/bundle-commit/v1` tag and the
explicit bundle encoding above.

## Merlin framing

Every proof transcript starts with its exact domain label from section 18.4,
then appends this common frame in order:

```text
protocol-id       = ASCII("BP52-DEAL-v1")
protocol-version  = u16le(1)
game-id           = Hash32
attempt           = u32le
role              = u8
prior-transcript  = Hash32
alice-identity    = 32-byte x-only key
bob-identity      = 32-byte x-only key
joint-key         = Point32
circuit-id        = Hash32
G                 = Point32
M                 = Point32
slot-count        = u16le(9)
```

Proof-specific statements are appended in canonical index order before their
temporary commitments. Every indexed record is preceded by its `u16`
little-endian index. Challenges are 64 bytes from Merlin under the label
`challenge`, reduced with `Scalar::from_bytes_mod_order_wide`, and are not
serialized. A zero challenge is rejected and proof generation retries with a
fresh nonce.

Scale statements encode offsets with `t_code`: `0=-52`, `1=0`, `2=+52`.
Identity restrictions are contextual: `PK`, `Y`, original `V`, original `R`,
and every scale-factor point `Q` must be nonidentity. Derived `S`, `D`, `E`, and
partial-decryption `Z` points are not rejected solely for being identity.

## Accepted deal

The accepted body order is:

```text
protocol_version || game_id || attempt || hashes_a[9] || hashes_b[9] ||
verification_transcript_root
```

`verification_transcript_root` is `T_16`, immediately after both valid
`DecryptOpen` envelopes and successful proof and zero-test verification. Each
party signs:

```text
TaggedSHA256("BP52/accepted-deal/v1", Encode(AcceptedDealBody))
```

The complete accepted deal appends Alice's signature and then Bob's. Signature
collection is deliberately outside `T_16`; including those signatures in the
root they sign would be circular.

## Circuit manifest

The manifest is canonical binary, not JSON or Serde. Its fixed order is:

```text
manifest_version || protocol_version || slot_count || base_length ||
maximum_length || sha256_gadget_id ByteVector || multiplier_count ||
linear_constraint_count || gens_capacity || backend_id ByteVector ||
source_revision ByteVector
```

All integers are little-endian with widths fixed by the implementation. ASCII
fields have compile-time maximum lengths. `circuit_id` is ordinary SHA-256 of
exactly these bytes. Adding or reordering a field changes the circuit ID.

## Retry and fault rules

After a verified collision, all secret state is erased before allocating the
next attempt. Incrementing `u32::MAX` is forbidden and requires a new game.
Live game state retains component-tagged public fingerprints for earlier
attempts and rejects reuse of every disclosed nonce, public key share, hash,
commitment, ciphertext component, scale output, decryption share, signature
nonce, and proof commitment. Points known to be pure multiples of `G` also
share one cross-kind fingerprint so scalar reuse cannot hide behind another
field type. The current accepted-deal structure does not bind prior-attempt
roots, so complete retry history is a live-state invariant rather than
something a third party can infer from `AcceptedDeal` alone.

Unauthenticated malformed traffic is discarded without blame. Authenticated
traffic for a different protocol version, game, attempt, sequence, or
predecessor is an out-of-context routing result: it does not advance or poison
the live attempt and is not blamed on its signer. A validly signed malformed
message at the exact current cursor faults its signer, and an explicitly
recorded timeout terminally faults the scheduled sender. Joint-key identity
and cross-party duplicate hashes are unattributable setup failures. The
state-machine error representation must therefore permit no blamed role.

The production lifecycle owns one live attempt and its concrete secret state.
Retry reasons and acceptance readiness are opaque capabilities emitted only by
semantic verification. Dropping a live or pending-acceptance value faults the
history, preventing the same `(game_id, attempt)` from being restarted with a
conflicting disposition. Old secrets are erased before the successor attempt
can be created.

## Privacy-preserving exceptional retry

A derived sum or difference ciphertext can, with negligible probability, have
an identity `R`. Its `S` component then permits brute-force recovery of the
small plaintext. The implementation performs a neutral, fresh-attempt retry if
any `SumCipher[i].R` or `D[k].R` is identity. This safeguard is not explicit in
the base specification and must be included in the next protocol revision or
otherwise resolved by its cryptographic review.
